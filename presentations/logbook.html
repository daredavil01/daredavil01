04Part I · v22.0.0 · the first logbook

The first version: six lenses in a row

The page launched as a new room in the site's Scriptorium. Everything the redesign kept was already there: the data, the six ideas and an Ask chat pinned to any essay.

  • A header with a row of lens chips.
  • A scrolly intro that was really a 24-second “film”: one dot per essay moving from a timeline to language lanes, to a map of meaning, to how loud each essay is — with Previous, Play and Next buttons and a seek bar.
  • Then a tab strip: Atlas, Sky, Fingerprint, Phrases, Letters, Found poems — one visible at a time.
  • A drawer beside the lens for the selected essay, its neighbours and the chat.
05Part I · what was in the way

Five of six ideas behind a tab

  • Hidden chapters. Only one lens showed at a time; five of the six waited behind a tab, with nothing on the page to show what they looked like.
  • A film with buttons. The intro's Play, Previous and Next duplicated what scrolling already did.
  • Shrunk on a phone. Every figure was a fixed viewBox scaled to fit; the 1,000 × 660 Atlas drew at 0.39× on a 390-px screen.
  • Very long walls. Fingerprint was a wall of 103 cards about 4,500 px tall; Letters a column of 22 pairs near 5,800 px.
  • Colour doing two jobs. Language was orange and teal in one place, region colours in another.
06Part I · v22.0.1 · the day-one fix

The nightly job was erasing the essays

The Logbook stands on the chatbot's index, so a bug there was a bug here. Eight minutes after launch came a patch.

Symptom

Every night the index lost 509 Substack body chunks that a local run had embedded.

Cause

Substack answers GitHub's runners with a 403 — API, bodies and RSS alike — so the nightly text cache held only the 29 WordPress essays, and indexing deleted the rest.

Fix

The cache lists what it lacks in missing; the essay source sits the run out with keep: true; npm run logbook keeps the last good file.

07Part II · The field journal

Bind it as a book, not a tab strip

The redesign treats the page as a field journal you read front to back: a cover, a short prologue, a contents page, six chapters, an index and a colophon. Any essay opens as a page of its own, slid over the chapter you found it in.

  • Cover — the route inked through every essay, in writing order, across the chart.
  • Prologue — five scrolled steps that turn a ledger into a map.
  • Contents — six chapters, each with a thumbnail drawn from the real data.
  • Chapters I–VI — Atlas, Sky, Fingerprint, Phrases, Letters, Found poems, with a floating dock to move between them.
  • Index & entry page — every essay in order; each one opens with a chat pinned to it.

Contents names each chapter with its Roman numeral and a miniature of what it shows. Once you are inside, a small dock floats at the bottom of the screen — ‹ I Atlas › — and follows whichever chapter is in view.

11Chapter I · Atlas

The chart of what I write about

“Essays sit beside the ones that say similar things — placed by the same multilingual embeddings the Ask page searches, laid flat — so an English essay and a Marathi one about the same hills share a coast.”

  • Search a title, tag or word; the rest of the chart fades back.
  • English / मराठी toggle, and a territory legend: hover to peek, click to isolate.
  • Hover a mark for a card with its first line; open it to pin its three nearest neighbours with dashed lines.
  • Walk the route plays the essays in writing order, 85 ms apiece, with a scrubber.
12Chapter I · Atlas

Islands grown from their own essays

The territories are not drawn by hand and not convex hulls. Each region's essays are painted as soft discs, and an SVG filter turns that cloud into land with a coastline — the same filter on the cover, the Atlas, the prologue and every entry page.

<!-- the "gooey" island, one per region -->
<feGaussianBlur stdDeviation={blur} />            1 · blur the discs
<feColorMatrix values="… 0 0 0 26 -11" /> 2 · threshold: land
<feMorphology operator="erode" radius="1.3"/> 3 · land minus it: coast
<feMorphology operator="dilate" />       4 · a ring offshore: shoal

Where two regions overlap they share a coast, which is the point: the map's borders are where one subject turns into another.

13Chapter II · Sky

Opening lines as constellations

“Every essay's first sentence, drawn after Nicholas Rougeux's Literary Constellations: one star per word, larger for longer words, turning hardest where the words carry meaning.”

  • 103 constellations in a twinkling field; year chips filter it.
  • Watch the sky fill lights them in writing order, with the year large behind.
  • The observatory draws one constellation star by star, each star labelled with its word.
  • The chapter is always night, in either theme. The share card's constellation and this one call the same function, constellationOf, so they draw the same shape.
14Chapter III · Fingerprint

Punctuation and rhythm, words removed

“Take the words away and an essay still has a shape.”

Each essay's punctuation is laid along a spiral in reading order — full stops in ink, commas in pencil, exclamation marks in vermilion, questions in teal — after Stefanie Posavec and Between the Words.

  • The specimen: one essay's spiral, its punctuation mix, a bar per sentence and four facts.
  • The cabinet: every spiral, filed by year. Sort by year, longest, loudest, most questions or calmest.
  • Hover to inspect, click to keep; on a phone a tap opens the essay.
15Chapter IV · Phrases

Habits of the hand

“The phrases I reach for without noticing. A phrase only counts once it turns up in several different essays, so these are habits, not subjects.”

The first version picked a line at random — and 280 of the 412 lines are from 2026, so always answering 2026 usually won. The game now draws a year first, then a line from it; it keeps a streak and says how many years off you were.

16Chapter V · Letters

My past self, answered

“A sentence from years ago, and the recent sentence closest to it in meaning. Nobody chose these pairs: the embeddings matched them, at least eighteen months apart.”

  • An arc diagram across the years: each arc joins a then to a now. Arrow keys walk the arcs; Enter opens one.
  • The pair is shown as two postmarked letters — Then and Now — with a ring for how close they are in meaning.
  • Previous, next and shuffle; pairs often cross languages, English answering Marathi.
17Chapter VI · Found poems

Lines cut from the archive

“Five sentences from five essays, shuffled until they read as one poem. Keep the lines you like and shuffle the rest.”

  • A padlock beside each line keeps it; Shuffle the others redraws the rest, inking new lines in.
  • The address bar is always a link to the poem in front of you (?poem= and ?lock=).
  • Copy link and Save as image — a PNG of the card.
  • Each line carries its year; tap it to open the essay it came from.
18§ Index & the entry page

Every essay, its own page

Click any mark in any chapter, or a row in the index, and the essay slides over as a page of the journal — a bottom sheet on a phone. ?post= holds it, so the page is a link.

  • Its photo, duotoned like every card on the site; its date, language and platform; its first line.
  • Words, reading time, average sentence and “! per 1,000”, then its fingerprint and rhythm.
  • A mini chart of where it sits, and its three nearest essays in meaning.
  • Ask about this essay — the site's chat, pinned to this essay and its neighbours.
  • ← and → turn the page in writing order; Esc closes and focus goes back where it was.
  • The § Index at the back lists all 103 — search, language, newest, oldest or longest — and a colophon says what is computed and what never ships.

21Part III · How it works

From essays to a chart, with no model call

The page reads one committed file, public/data/logbook.json. npm run logbook builds it from text and vectors the site already has for its chatbot, so the Logbook adds no new model calls at all.

1 · Text

Every essay

Substack and WordPress, cached in the gitignored knowledge_base/ by the word-count job.

2 · Vectors

Chunk embeddings

The /ask index already holds bge-m3 vectors per chunk; each essay gets their mean.

3 · Layout

UMAP to 2D

umap-js, seeded: 40 neighbours and a loose minDist of 0.8, so ~100 essays don't scatter into far islands.

4 · Regions

k-means, named by tags

k = 6 on the flat layout, each named after its essays' two commonest topical tags.

5 · Ship

logbook.json

177 KB, under a 200 KB budget a test enforces.

It runs as the logbook step of npm run refresh, after ask:index. An essay with no chunks yet is left off the chart, never placed at the origin.

22Part III · How it works

Two languages, one map

bge-m3 is multilingual, so a Marathi essay about a Sahyadri fort and an English one about the same climb land next to each other. Language is a property of the mark, never of the map.

  • Detection by share of letters: a text is Marathi when its Devanagari letters reach half its Latin ones, so an English essay quoting one Marathi word is still English — and a title keeps its own language.
  • Doubled by shape: English is an indigo circle, Marathi a saffron diamond, in every chapter — colour alone never carries the meaning.
  • Set in its own type: Devanagari in Tiro Devanagari Marathi beside Instrument Serif, with lang="mr" for screen readers.

23Part III · How it works

Sentences may ship. Paragraphs never do.

The essays live on Substack and WordPress; the Logbook only points at them. So the committed JSON may carry single sentences — each linking back to its essay — and nothing longer.

// src/lib/logbook.js — the rule
export const MAX_EXCERPT_WORDS = 30;

// src/lib/logbook.test.js — the guard
it("stays under 200 KB", () =>
  expect(raw.length).toBeLessThan(200 * 1024));

The test holds the committed file to both: no excerpt over 30 words, no file over 200 KB. The full text stays in the gitignored knowledge_base/. A line must also start like a sentence — a capital, a quote or a Devanagari letter — never on a digit.

25Part III · How it works

Rules the page keeps

Real pixel width

No scaled viewBox

A useWidth hook (a ResizeObserver) gives every figure its real width; SVG type is set in pixels, so it stays readable on a phone.

Container queries

The box, not the screen

Layouts switch on .lb-cq container width, because the classic shell's sidebar makes viewport breakpoints lie.

Tokens

One palette, two themes

Every colour is a --lb-* variable in logbook.css; dark mode redefines them, so no SVG needs a dark variant.

URL state

Every view is a link

?post= an essay, ?lens= a chapter, ?poem= + ?lock= a poem, ?tour the film — always merged, so ?view= survives.

Keyboard

One tab stop per field

Marks use a roving tabindex: arrows move in writing order, Home/End jump, Enter opens. Dialogs trap focus and give it back.

Weight

Fonts on this page only

Instrument Serif and Tiro Devanagari Marathi load through Helmet on /logbook alone; the chapter bundle is about 28 KB gzipped.

28Part IV · What the screenshots caught

The Marathi essays vanished

The first screenshot of the new Atlas showed only circles: all seventeen saffron diamonds had gone. Caught before it shipped.

Symptom

Every Marathi mark was off the chart. The English circles were fine.

Cause

The diamonds were rects with an SVG rotate() attribute. The new hover scale needed CSS transform-origin: center, which re-anchored that rotation and flung them away.

Fix

Draw a diamond as a polygon — diamond(cx, cy, r) in shared.js — now a rule in the repo's CLAUDE.md.

export const diamond = (cx, cy, r) =>
  `${cx},${cy - r} ${cx + r},${cy} ${cx},${cy + r} ${cx - r},${cy}`;
29Part IV · What the screenshots caught

Small things a reader would notice

Symptom

A game line began “५० वाजता…” — half a time of day.

Cause

The sentence splitter broke at the decimal point in “६.५० वाजता”, leaving a fragment that starts on a digit — one line in 412.

Fix

startsLikeSentence() in the analysis, plus a filter for the committed data; a test now says no line starts on a digit, in either script.

Symptom

Region names disappeared under the dots.

Cause

Labels were drawn in the ground layer, beneath the marks.

Fix

A separate RegionLabels layer, drawn after the marks, with a paper halo.

Symptom

The cover's stat read “EXCLAMATIO N”, and the “Latest” label was clipped at the edge.

Cause

Wide letter-spacing plus overflow-wrap broke the word; the label had no room on the right.

Fix

Tighter kicker spacing and normal wrapping; the label flips to the left near the edge.

30Part IV · What the screenshots caught

Save as image gave a black card

Symptom

The exported PNG was solid black behind the poem, and shifted by its margin.

Cause

html-to-image draws the paper-grain data URI as black, and bakes the node's margins into the canvas.

Fix

A lb-poem--flat class while it captures (plain paper, no grain), and the margin moved onto a wrapper.

Verified by actually downloading the file in a headless browser, not by trusting the code path.

35Part V · how it was made

A browser for a camera, ffmpeg for an editor

1 · Voice

Piper TTS

en_GB-cori-high, a little faster than default; 62 s of voice, 182 words.

2 · Camera

A scripted browser

Playwright at 414 × 690, 2× pixels; a CDP screencast saved 2,919 timed frames as it hovered, clicked and scrolled.

3 · Clock

Even 30 fps

Frames arrive unevenly (~36 a second); each 30 fps tick takes the newest frame before it.

4 · Frame

1080 × 1920

The recording in a rounded window on paper, a title and caption per scene, an end card.

5 · Encode

Two cuts

A 22.4 MB master to share; a 720 × 1280 web encode of 4.3 MB for the page.

The Ask button and theme toggles were hidden while recording, and a vermilion ring shows each tap. A 19-cue WebVTT and an .srt carry the narration as text.

36Part V · what the meter caught

The music was nearly silent

Nobody listened to the first mix — the work happened in a container with no speakers — so it was measured instead.

Symptom

Between narration lines the track measured about −62 dB: effectively silence.

Cause

The loop is a quiet file (−40.9 dB mean), mixed at 0.55 and ducked hard under the voice.

Fix

+16 dB on the bed, gentler ducking, loudness normalised to −16 LUFS.

volume=16dB,
sidechaincompress=threshold=0.02:ratio=6:attack=40:release=600,
loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-1.5:LRA=11

Gaps now sit at −43 to −34 dB under a voice at −18.5 dB; peak −2.6 dBFS.
