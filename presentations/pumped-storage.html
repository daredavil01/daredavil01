02Purpose · the question

Not whether India needs storage, but where it goes

A pumped storage plant is two reservoirs and a tunnel. At midday, when solar power is cheap, it pumps water uphill; in the evening, when power is dear, it lets the water fall back through turbines. Maharashtra is planning dozens of them, most on forested Sahyadri hilltops.

The site asks what each one costs the forest and the people who live around it, and sets that beside what it gives the grid.

03Purpose · the order of things

Trees first, and every number checkable

The story is told in a fixed order of importance, and it has to hold up when a developer, an official or a neighbour checks it.

  1. The forest. The trees and the whole Sahyadri ecosystem: species found nowhere else, corridors, sacred groves.
  2. The process. How environmental reviews are sidestepped, and how projects are imposed on local and Adivasi communities.
  3. The energy case. Power, emissions and cost, as supporting evidence — including what supporters say.

Independent

No money from developers, governments or parties. Built from official documents and 75 vetted outlets.

Labelled evidence

Every claim is Documented Reported Alleged or an Official claim. Allegations are always attributed by name.

What you can do

  1. Know the facts. Read the story and check the projects near your district.
  2. Go to the gram sabha. Under PESA and the Forest Rights Act, forest land in many villages cannot be diverted without its consent.
  3. Object at the public hearing. Send written objections before the date and keep a stamped copy.
  4. Ask for the papers. File an RTI for the study terms, EIA report, forest proposal and tree count.
  5. Write to the decision makers. The expert committees, the collector, your MLA and MP, naming the Parivesh proposal number.
  6. Stand with forest-rights claims. Help families file and follow up individual and community claims.
  7. Document what you see. Photograph surveys, drilling or felling with date and place.
  8. Share, and correct. Share pages on WhatsApp and point people to the source.
  9. Volunteer. Help the groups with surveys, translation, legal work or outreach.

Lawful, peaceful action only. The site is independent and not affiliated with the groups listed; their contact details are on the site’s Take action page.

Home, and a story in ten chapters

Home opens on a Sahyadri photo and four headline numbers, then an animated explainer, How a hill becomes a battery: cheap daytime power, dear evening power, the cost to the forest, the cost to life around it. A fair box says what pumped storage is good for, and cards lead to every page.

07The pages · 2 of 5

Projects: 39 dots on one ridge

Every pumped storage project planned in Maharashtra, 60,615 MW in all, in one table and on one map.

  • The table is sorted by trees to be felled, then forest land, then capacity — never by megawatts first. Filter by status, or show Western Ghats projects only.
  • The map (Leaflet on OpenStreetMap) places each project from its coordinates; dot size is MW, colour is the clearance stage.
  • A page per project: MW, trees, forest hectares, villages, a progress bar from MoU to operation, its timeline, its documents and a WhatsApp button.
08The pages · 3 of 5

Timeline and news, every morning

Timeline: 89 events — MoUs, study terms, clearances, felling orders, hearings and protests — newest first. It opens on the “Forest & people” filter, and can be narrowed by year, category, project or verified-only.

News: reports from a fixed list of 75 vetted outlets, newest first, with tag filters that put trees, ecosystem and consent first. A daily sync adds new items; anything not yet checked by a person carries a not yet reviewed badge. The same list is the /news.xml RSS feed.

09The pages · 4 of 5

Shortcuts, and the voices they skip

Shortcuts: 13 cards, each naming a safeguard in environmental or forest law, what happened instead, and the documents that show it. Example: the land law requires a resettlement plan, but Karjat’s draft EIA calls it “not applicable” by citing a 1,000 ha threshold — for a project that needs 377 ha. Filter by project; 7 are shown in official documents.

Voices: 14 records from the affected villages — gram sabha resolutions, public hearings and objections — attributed by name, with an evidence label on each.

10The pages · 5 of 5

How the numbers are worked out

Methodology: 11 inputs and every formula behind the model — forest per GWh stored, round-trip loss (100 units in, about 77.5 out), emissions on today’s grid, and battery vs pumped storage tariffs from recent tenders. It is labelled a model, not a measurement, and every input names its source.

Glossary: 44 terms in plain words — ToR, EC, FC, ESA, gram sabha — grouped by category, each with its own link.

About: the source policy, the full outlet list, corrections, the right of reply to developers, and the data downloads.

11How it was made · the sources

One row for every number

No number is typed into the copy. Each one is a token that reads a row in data/citations.json, and the build fails if a token doesn’t resolve.

  • 466 citation rows: 448 quoted word for word, 13 derived from other rows, and 5 still flagged (2 unlocated, 3 unreachable).
  • Each row keeps the document, its PDF page, the exact sentence and an evidence label. The ⓘ bubble shows it and links to url#page=N.
  • Sources are official documents first — all 42 forest committee minutes on Parivesh, expert committee minutes, MPCB EIA summaries, clearances — then 75 vetted outlets, 50 of them allowed for the daily sync.
  • Checking every number against its page corrected 6 figures, including Karjat’s tree count.

12How it was made · the build

Git is the database; a build is the check

  1. Storage — one JSON file per entity in data/: projects, timeline, news, shortcuts, consent, ecology, outlets, glossary, facts, citations. Every change is a commit.
  2. Data layer — zod schemas are the one definition of every shape; loaders, cross-file integrity checks, selectors, the energy model and the { en, mr } text helpers sit on top.
  3. Pages — Astro renders each page once per language into one static URL. A schema error fails the build, so bad data never ships.
  4. Daily sync — a Claude scheduled task runs the sync-ghats-data skill at 07:40 IST: search whitelisted outlets, dedupe, add as unreviewed, validate, commit.
  5. Hosting — Cloudflare Pages rebuilds on every push.
