Tier one

The site

What is on it, what it does, who can change it, and what the AI does for them. No technical background assumed.

Site map साइटचा नकाशा

Sixteen public pages in four groups, plus the files machines read and the door staff use.

What each page does प्रत्येक पान

One line each, roughly in the order a visitor meets them.

  • /Home. The Marathi tagline as the headline (in one of two hero layouts the admin switches between), the impact figures counting up, the mission beside a photograph, the first six programmes as cards with their icons, the latest three news stories, and a closing band asking readers to give or volunteer.
  • /aboutThe foundation's story in three parts with its photograph and caption, the mission, a list of key facts, and the values.
  • /programsEvery published programme as a card with its cover photograph and its icon.
  • /programs/:slugOne programme in full: the write-up — headings, lists, quotes and photographs placed between paragraphs — the region, the focus area, the year it started, and a donate prompt.
  • /satpuda"Come to Satpuda": the three steps to arranging a visit, what to expect, and testimonials from people who have been — with their photographs and links to the related blog post, a Facebook post, a YouTube video or any other page.
  • /eventsUpcoming, ongoing and past events, sorted on every visit from the current Indian date so nothing is ever stale. An event can carry an "Open in Google Maps ↗" link.
  • /galleryOne tab per category and, inside each, photographs grouped into albums — three per group, the rest behind "Show more photos". Search the captions, pick an album, sort by newest or oldest; tap a photograph to enlarge it.
  • /blogPosts with a cover, a category, an excerpt, the author and the date.
  • /blog/:slugOne post in full, with photographs between paragraphs, a "Share on WhatsApp" button and a prompt to donate at the end.
  • /newsCoverage of the foundation by others — newspaper cuttings, online articles, TV and video. Tabs by type; search, year, outlet and sort. Each story opens in a detail view with its own link and share buttons. A video is a link to YouTube, not an embed.
  • /teamThree groups — advisory, core and fellows — each with its own empty state, and a drawn silhouette where there is no photograph.
  • /resourcesDocuments to download. A resource with no file attached shows as pending rather than as a broken link.
  • /donateSuggested amounts, how to give, the UPI QR code, bank details, and the registration numbers donors need for tax. Each block appears only when its own details are filled in.
  • /contactEmail, phone numbers and office addresses; "Follow us" icons for WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram and YouTube; the trust's registration number; and a message form.
  • /volunteerThe roles available, other ways to help, and a sign-up form asking for a name and at least one way to reply.
  • /legal/…Privacy, terms and an accessibility statement, as three tabs. The contact address in them is read from the site settings, so it cannot drift from the real one.

A page never shows a blank where a fact is missing. Every block on /donate, /about and /satpuda is tied to its own data and disappears when that data is absent. An empty "80G —" line would read as a claim the foundation cannot back, and donors rely on those numbers, so the whole line is suppressed instead. Links follow the same rule: a social icon appears only when its address is set, a Maps link only when an event has one, and a testimonial's "Read the story" only while that post is published.

Found and shared शेअर आणि शोध

Most visitors arrive from a forwarded WhatsApp message or a search. Both are designed for.

  • A picture in every preview

    A page with a photograph of its own — a programme, a post, a news story — shares that photograph as a large card, with its alt text. Every other page shares the foundation's logo, which is also the icon in the browser tab and on a phone's home screen.

  • Sharing without anyone else's code

    "Share on WhatsApp" is a plain link with the message already written: a post uses its own WhatsApp message field, a news story builds one from its headline, outlet, date and summary. "Share on Facebook" is a plain link too. Nothing reaches either company until the reader taps.

  • A shared story opens on that story

    /news?item=N renders the news page with that story already open, and the page's title, description and preview image become the story's — so the preview in a chat shows the cutting itself.

  • Two addresses per page, both findable

    Each page tells search engines its canonical address and its twin in the other language. The sitemap is written from the database, so a new programme is listed the moment it is published and a draft never is.

  • Links, not embeds

    YouTube videos and Google Maps are links that open in their own apps. No page loads Google's or YouTube's code, and the privacy notice's list of providers stays short.

  • Counted without cookies

    Visits are counted by Cloudflare's cookieless analytics, so there is no consent banner to dismiss. The admin and the editors' preview are not counted.

Two languages दोन भाषा

Marathi is the site's own language. English is the translation — not the other way round.

  • Switching is a link

    The toggle in the header is an ordinary link to ?lang=en. It works with JavaScript off, the choice is remembered for a year, and each language version has its own shareable, searchable address.

  • Everything is translatable

    Every piece of text on the site — page copy, menu labels, dates, button words — exists in both languages. Dates are formatted by hand rather than by the browser, and digits are Latin in both, so numbers read the same to everyone.

  • Half-translated is a supported state

    A programme can be written in Marathi and published before anyone has translated it. An English reader sees the Marathi with a quiet (मराठी) marker rather than a gap.

What staff can edit संपादन

Twenty-three tabs behind a login at /admin, every one drawn from a single registry of fields. Between them they cover every word and every photograph on the public site — there is nothing an editor has to ask a developer to change. The admin itself switches between Marathi and English, because the people editing it are in Dhule and Nandurbar.

  • Single-record forms 3

    One long form each, for settings that exist exactly once.

    • Site & hero — titles, tagline, hero copy and its layout, logo, email, phones, and the WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram and YouTube links
    • About page — the story paragraphs, mission and story photograph
    • Donation info — UPI ID, QR code, bank details, 80G / 12A / trust registration / PAN, the domestic-only notice

  • Page text 1

    The 73 lines of the foundation's own words that sit around the content — every page's kicker, title and introduction, the Satpuda steps, the ways to volunteer, the donate how-to. All on one screen, grouped by page; one Save writes only the lines that changed.

  • Ordered lists 12

    Add, edit, reorder, publish or unpublish rows. Order on the page is the order here.

    • Offices · Donation amounts · Key facts · Values · Impact stats
    • Programs · Blog · News & media · Events
    • Team · Testimonials · Resources

  • Gallery 3

    Gallery categories are the page's tabs, Gallery albums group photographs inside a category, and Gallery places each photograph. Unpublishing a category or an album hides everything inside it, not just its heading.

  • Photos & media 1

    Every image, video and document in one library: search, filter, bulk publish, alt text and captions in both languages, several sections per photograph, and a "Used in" line naming every page a file appears on before anyone deletes it.

  • Inboxes 2

    Contact messages and volunteer sign-ups, read-only. The public can add to these and nobody outside the login can read them back.

  • AI 1

    Which provider and model the AI helpers use, a fallback, the prices used to estimate cost, a Test button, and a year of usage by task, model and person. Only admins can change it.

What editing feels like

  • Both languages, side by side

    Every translatable field shows a Marathi box and an English box together. There is no separate "translate this page" mode to remember to visit.

  • A real editor for long text

    Programme and blog bodies get headings, bold and italic, lists, quotes, dividers, links, and photographs from the library placed between paragraphs with a caption. Pasting from Word or a web page keeps the text and drops what the site cannot show.

  • "Appears on", and the real page beside the form

    Every tab says where its rows show on the site. "See it on the site" opens that page beside the editor, with the row outlined and unsaved typing already in it — in Marathi or English.

  • Nothing is public until it is published

    New rows start unpublished, and so do new photographs. Picking a draft photograph for a page is flagged in amber at the moment it happens, because a draft renders as a placeholder and the editor would otherwise never know.

  • Photographs shrink before they upload

    A phone picture is resized and re-encoded in the browser, and the form shows the saving before anything is sent — "3.8 MB → 184 KB". SVGs, animated GIFs and images already small go up untouched, which keeps the donation QR code exact.

  • Words while it waits

    Anything slow shows a turning ✻ and a changing verb — "Marinating…", "Reticulating…". A screen reader hears the plain label once instead, and the mark stops turning for anyone who has asked for less motion.

The AI helpers AI ची मदत

Eight helpers in the admin. Every one of them writes a draft into the form; none of them saves anything. The editor reads the draft, changes it, and presses Save — or presses Undo.

  • Draft English · Draft MarathiTranslation in both directions, on every bilingual field and on a photograph's alt text and caption. "Draft Marathi" appears whenever the English half has text.
  • Write · Expand · ShortenWriting help under each bilingual field, with an optional one-line note ("mention the Dhadgaon school"). The rest of the row is the context, and nothing else is sent.
  • Fill empty fieldsOne press drafts every empty field of a programme, post, event or value in both languages, from what the row already says. It refuses a row with no text at all — "write a title first" — because a draft from nothing is invention.
  • Describe with AIFor one photograph in the library: alt text and a caption in both languages, and up to three sections it belongs in.
  • Describe photos missing alt textThe same, for up to fifty photographs at a time, paced one every few seconds, with a counter and a Stop button. The results arrive as a list to tick; a save fills only what was empty.
  • Suggest from libraryOn the gallery tabs: a category and album for each photograph not yet in the gallery, plus any new categories and albums worth creating. Everything it creates is a draft.
  • Read a news clippingOn News & media: reads the chosen photograph of a cutting or article and fills in the headline and outlet as printed and translated, a short summary, the type, and the date — only if one is printed.
  • Pull news from the libraryReads library photographs one at a time and proposes a draft news item for each that is coverage. It skips photographs already used, and a headline that matches an existing story is flagged "looks already added" and left unticked.

The rules every helper keeps

  • A draft, never a decision

    A result lands in the form with one level of Undo. It fills only fields that are still empty when it arrives, so nothing an editor typed meanwhile is overwritten. Bulk results go to a review list, and whatever is created from them starts as a draft.

  • Quotes and figures are translate-only

    A testimonial's quote, a key fact's value, an impact figure's label and a news story's headline and outlet can be translated but never rewritten. Nor can the page text. The server checks this again rather than trusting the button.

  • No invented numbers

    Every number in a draft is compared with what the model was given, and any that were not in it are listed under the field — "check them before saving: 2015". Digits written in Devanagari are turned into Latin ones, as the site uses everywhere.

  • Photographs, not people

    A description covers only what is visible. It never identifies anyone, never guesses age, caste, tribe or religion, and never guesses a place, date or event unless it is written in the picture.

  • Nothing from nothing

    A cutting with no printed date comes back with no date, and its row cannot be ticked until someone types one. A photograph that is not news comes back empty rather than as a plausible story.

  • Garbled script is refused

    Marathi containing another Indic script — a model once mixed Bengali into an alt text — is dropped, so the field shows "needs translation" instead of a convincing mistake.

Built for phones on slow networks फोनसाठी

The audience is in the Satpuda hills — Dhadgaon and Shirpur, in Nandurbar and Dhule districts. Most of them arrive on a phone, on a patchy connection.

  • Finished HTMLPublic pages arrive already built and download no script bundle, so they render on a slow connection and an old phone, and they work if a script fails. The menu, the photo viewer and the news story view are HTML and CSS.
  • Split fontsThe Marathi typeface is 121 KB. English pages do not download it — including the two Devanagari characters in the logo and the language toggle, which get their own 2.5 KB file for exactly that reason.
  • Capped imagesEvery uploaded photograph is resized to 1600px and squeezed under 250 KB before it is stored, because what a photo costs to serve is fixed at upload time, not at display time.
  • Photos on demandThe gallery shows three photographs per group. The rest are hidden until "Show more" is opened, and a hidden photograph is not downloaded until then.
  • Filters are addressesSearch and filters on News and Gallery are ordinary forms, so every view has its own link and works with scripting off. Picking from a dropdown applies at once.
  • 44px targetsNothing tappable is smaller than that at any screen width, the editor's toolbar included. A layout that does not fit gets changed; the button does not get smaller.
  • AccessibilityWCAG 2.1 AA is the target. One main heading per page, text tagged with the language it is actually in, and an accessibility statement published at /legal/accessibility.

Tier two

How it is built

The stack, the data model, the AI layer, and the decisions that shape everything else. Written for whoever maintains this next.

The stack तंत्रज्ञान

Read out of package.json and astro.config.mjs.

  • Astro 7server-rendered, no static build
  • React 19admin only, never on a public page
  • Tiptap 3the body editor, loaded only when opened
  • SupabasePostgres, storage and sign-in
  • Cloudflare Workersthe deploy runtime
  • TypeScriptstrict, with null checks
  • Turnstilebot check on the two public forms
  • Resendemails the office about a new submission
  • Web AnalyticsCloudflare's, cookieless, public pages only
  • Gemini · Anthropic · AgentRouterthe admin's AI providers

Server-rendered rather than statically built, on purpose. Every word of page furniture — the menu labels, the site title, the page text — comes out of the database, so a static build would have to be regenerated every time an editor changed a comma. The cost is that every request reads the database, a known and recorded trade.

The development server and the production runtime are different engines. npm run dev runs on Node; production runs on Cloudflare's workerd, which has no DOM, no Image and no canvas. Code that works on one can fail on the other, so any server-side change is verified with npm run build && npm run preview, which runs the real runtime locally. It is also why photographs are resized in the browser: it is the only machine in the path that can.

What happens on a request विनंतीचा प्रवास

One page view, from the tap to the HTML. Everything below happens inside a single Cloudflare Worker before any markup is sent.

The data model माहितीची रचना

Twenty-six tables, grouped by what they are for, from eighteen migrations.

  • Settings, exactly one row each 4

    • site_settings — titles, hero, contacts, socials
    • about_page — story and mission copy
    • donation_info — UPI, bank, registrations
    • ai_settings — provider, model, fallback, prices

    Constrained to a single row in the schema, so "there is only one" is a fact about the database rather than a convention.

  • Content 16

    • programs · blog_posts · events · news_items
    • gallery_categories · gallery_albums · gallery_items
    • team_members · testimonials · resources
    • key_facts · org_values · impact_stats
    • offices · donation_asks · page_copy

  • Media 1

    media — one row per image, video or document, with its alt text and caption in both languages, its size, and the sections it belongs to. Everything else points at it.

  • Inboxes 3

    • contact_messages
    • volunteer_submissions
    • event_registrations

    The public can insert and cannot read — not even the row they just wrote. Consent is a column the database refuses to accept as false.

  • People and the AI log 2

    • profiles — each staff account's role
    • ai_usage — one row per AI attempt

    Plus one view, ai_usage_monthly, which totals the log by month in the database, because the API returns at most 1,000 rows at a time.

Rules the schema enforces rather than documents. Every content table carries its own sort_order and an is_published flag that defaults to off, so nothing is ever accidentally live. No table has a path column — there is no cover_path anywhere, because a path is something you can type wrongly and a foreign key is not. And every web address an editor can type is checked by the database: WhatsApp, Facebook, YouTube and Google Maps links must be https on those companies' own hosts, and a testimonial's extra links must be https with no spaces. A javascript: link is refused for every writer, not just by this form.

Relationships the database keeps. An album and its photographs share a category through a two-column foreign key, so a photograph cannot sit in an album from another category, and moving an album moves its photographs. A category still in use cannot be deleted. A testimonial links to a blog post by ID, so renaming the post's address cannot break the link, and deleting the post only detaches it.

Derived facts are not stored. An event has a start date and an end date and no status column; whether it is upcoming, ongoing or past is worked out on every render from the Indian date. A stored status is a fact that goes wrong quietly at midnight.

Who can read what प्रवेश नियंत्रण

Permission is enforced by the database on every statement, not by the code that asks.

  • SettingsReadable by anyone — there is nothing private in them — except the AI settings, which only staff can read and only admins can change.
  • ContentReadable by anyone if published. A draft is invisible to the public site and 404s by direct URL. Page text has no drafts and is always readable.
  • ParentsA gallery photograph is public only if its media row, its album and its category are all published too. Unpublishing any one of them hides it.
  • InboxesAnyone may add a row. Nobody outside the login may read one — there is no read policy at all, which is stronger than a policy that returns nothing.
  • AI logA writer may add rows only under their own account; only admins read the log. The public cannot see it or the monthly view.
  • StaffA signed-in writer may read and write content, checked against a role stored in the database rather than a claim in the token. A viewer can look but cannot save or use the AI.

The all-powerful database key is used nowhere. It is declared to the build as server-only and secret, so importing it into anything the browser sees is a build error rather than a review comment. On top of that, continuous integration searches the built output on every push for six kinds of leaked key — both formats of the database key, and the Anthropic, Google (old and new format) and AgentRouter key shapes — and fails the build if it finds one.

Media is referenced by ID ओळख क्रमांकाने

The part of this codebase least like the obvious version, and the one the most features now stand on.

One reference deletes, the rest detach. Removing a photograph from the library empties every cover, icon and slot that used it — except a gallery tile, which is nothing without its photograph and is deleted with it. The library's delete warning says which, per page, before anyone confirms.

Where an editor's save goes बदल कुठे जातात

There is no content API. The one admin endpoint drafts text; it never saves a page.

The AI layer AI ची रचना

Eight helpers, six tasks, one endpoint: /api/admin/ai. Everything the helpers do passes through it, and it is the only place a provider key is ever used.

The six tasks

  • translateMarathi to English or back, for one field or one photograph's alt text and caption. Rich text comes back as HTML and is sanitised before it touches the editor.
  • rewriteWrite, Expand or Shorten one bilingual field. The context is built from the tab's own registry fields only — inbox, media and AI tabs are never sent — and the endpoint re-checks that the field is not translate-only.
  • fillOne call for every empty writable field on a row, in both languages. Refused when the row has no text to draft from.
  • describeAlt text, caption and sections for one photograph. The bulk "missing alt text" run calls this once per photograph, paced, and waits and retries once on a rate limit.
  • clippingHeadline, outlet, summary, type and printed date from a photograph of coverage. "Pull news from the library" calls this once per photograph over a chosen range, skipping any already used by a story.
  • galleryCategory and album suggestions for up to 150 photographs per call. It sends the photographs' descriptions, not the images, so a suggestion costs a few hundred tokens; undescribed photographs are counted and left out.

  • Photographs are fetched by the server, by ID

    For describe and clipping the browser sends only a media ID. The server looks up the row under the editor's session and fetches the file itself — JPEG, PNG or WebP, at most 1.5 MB — so the browser can never point the AI at an arbitrary address.

  • Keys are secrets, not settings

    The provider keys, the backup Gemini key and AgentRouter's base address are Cloudflare secrets. None is a column an admin can edit: an editable address is one an admin could point at their own server, and the key would follow it.

  • Chosen, tested and counted in the AI tab

    Model names are suggested from each provider's live list. A Test button calls exactly what is typed, with no fallback, so a test never reports the wrong model as working. Usage shows twelve months of totals, a month by task, model and person, an estimated cost, and the last twenty failures.

  • Checked in code, not just asked for in prompts

    Each prompt states its rules, and each rule a model broke on a real run became a check in code as well — the number list, the digit conversion, the script filter, the caption and slug rules. /api/health runs fixtures through the number, digit, script and gallery checks on every call.

The JavaScript budget JavaScript चा हिशोब

Spent where it earns its place, and measured from the build rather than estimated.

The public site

  • BundlesNone, on any page. The mobile menu is a native disclosure widget, the photo viewer and the news story view are CSS reacting to the address bar, and the language toggle is a link — all keyboard- and screen-reader-correct for free.
  • Home · 468 BInline, for the impact figures counting up — and only when there are figures to count. The framework version of the same effect costs 184 KB.
  • News · 334 BInline, only while a story is open: it reveals a "Share" button where the phone can share or copy. Without it, the WhatsApp, Facebook and plain links remain.
  • FiltersOne onchange attribute on each filter form, so a dropdown applies when picked. With scripting off, the Apply button does the same.
  • Cloudflare'sThe Turnstile check on the contact and volunteer forms, and the cookieless analytics beacon — both loaded deferred, neither on the admin.

The admin, behind the login

  • First load504 KB (143 KB compressed): the React runtime 184 KB, Astro's integration 8 KB, the tab shell 41 KB, and the shared forms-and-library chunk 270 KB.
  • Body editor405 KB (126 KB compressed) more, fetched only when a rich-text field opens, so the twenty tabs without one never carry it.
  • Preview scriptInside the preview frame only, and only for a signed-in writer.

The admin pays what the public site does not. It sits behind a login, is used by a handful of staff, and caches after the first visit, so its size is a deliberate trade. Every change to it is measured off the build, with and without the change, and the difference is written down.

How it stays honest तपासण्या

The checks that run without anyone remembering to run them, and the habits behind the rest.

  • On every pushType-check, format check, build, then a search of the built output for six shapes of leaked key. Any one failing stops the deploy.
  • /api/healthNine checks — database, sign-in, languages, content, media, schema, forms, AI and body photographs — each written so that it can actually fail. A missing optional key is a warning, not an outage, so the check never gets deleted for blocking a deploy.
  • npm run probe:rlsSigns in as nobody and asserts what can and cannot be read, table by table, against the real database — including that drafts, inboxes and the AI log stay invisible.
  • Every migrationRun first inside a transaction that is thrown away, with checks under simulated roles; then a dry run that must list only that file; then applied. The recorded history matches production, 0001–0018.
  • On the real runtimeServer changes are exercised under workerd with npm run preview, not only under Node — and anything visual is checked in a screenshot, because a passing build once shipped an invisible home-page heading.
